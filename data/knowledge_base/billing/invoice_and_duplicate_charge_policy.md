# Invoice Disputes and Duplicate Charge Policy

This policy covers invoice questions, duplicate card charges, failed renewal attempts, and refund routing for enterprise subscriptions. Support agents may explain invoice line items and collect evidence, but only Billing Operations can approve a refund or account credit.

## Duplicate charges

First determine whether the customer sees two settled charges or one settled charge plus a pending authorization. Pending authorizations usually disappear according to the issuing bank's schedule and must not be described as completed charges. Collect the invoice number, transaction dates, amounts, currencies, and the last four card digits. Never request a full card number or security code.

Search for separate invoices, subscription upgrades, seat true-ups, and retried payments. If two settled transactions refer to the same invoice and amount, create a high-priority billing case and attach the payment processor reference IDs. Billing Operations should acknowledge the case within four business hours.

## Invoice corrections

Legal entity names, purchase-order references, and billing addresses can be corrected when the customer provides authorized account details. Tax identification changes require review by Billing Operations and may not be backdated where local regulations prohibit it. Do not promise that a corrected invoice will alter tax already collected.

## Refund expectations

Approved refunds are submitted to the original payment method. Processing typically takes five to ten business days after approval, but the customer's bank controls final posting. If the original method cannot receive a refund, escalate to Billing Operations instead of collecting alternate bank information in a support ticket.

Escalate immediately when a customer reports unknown charges, suspected payment-account compromise, or a chargeback notice. Mark these cases as security-sensitive and limit copied personal information.
